AI engines parse and cite content differently from Google. A few practical
rules to keep in mind when publishing.

## Lead with the answer

AI engines summarize the first 2–3 paragraphs and decide whether to cite
you based on whether the answer is *there*. Don&apos;t bury the lede in a
personal anecdote or product pitch.

**Bad** opening:

> *Last summer I was running a marathon and ...*

**Good** opening:

> Choose a road running shoe with 8–10mm heel-toe drop and 200–300g weight
> for most marathon runners. Top picks for 2026: ...

## Use H2 and H3 questions

Headings that end in `?` get auto-promoted to FAQPage schema (see
[FAQ Setup](/docs/faq-setup)). They&apos;re also the strongest signal that
the next paragraph contains an answer the AI engine can lift verbatim.

## Cite your sources inline

AI engines look for outbound citations as a trust signal. If you reference
a study, link to it. If you compare products, link to the manufacturer.

## Keep paragraphs short

3–4 sentences max. Long paragraphs get truncated when AI engines extract
snippets.

## Add structured comparisons

Tables and bulleted lists are easy for AI engines to lift. A pricing or
feature comparison table will get cited more often than the same content
in prose.

## Update old content

AI engines weight recency. A post with `2024` in the title that hasn&apos;t
been touched in two years will be deprioritized. Update + republish
annually for evergreen content.
